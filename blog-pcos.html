Understanding PCOS: Symptoms, Root Causes & Practical Management

🩺 Medically Reviewed by Dr. Kiran Sarda (MBBS, DGO)
• ⏱️ 5 min read • Solapur, Maharashtra

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most widespread hormonal conditions among women of reproductive age. In India, recent epidemiological studies estimate that nearly 1 in every 5 women experiences symptoms of PCOS. Yet, because symptoms can vary dramatically from one person to another, many women spend months or even years wondering why their periods are irregular, why losing weight feels unusually difficult, or why sudden acne breakouts keep recurring.

At Sarda Hospital, our goal is to dispel the fear and myths surrounding PCOS. It is a manageable condition, not a life sentence. Here is an evidence-based medical guide to understanding what is happening in your body and what practical steps you can take today.

What Exactly Is PCOS?

PCOS is an endocrine (hormonal) and metabolic disorder. In a typical menstrual cycle, the ovaries develop several small follicles, one of which matures and releases a healthy egg (ovulation).

In women with PCOS, a communication mismatch occurs between the brain (the pituitary gland) and the ovaries. The ovaries produce slightly elevated levels of androgens (hormones often described as male hormones, although all women naturally produce them in small quantities). Because of this hormonal imbalance, the small follicles remain immature and do not release an egg regularly. On an ultrasound scan, these tiny, harmless, fluid-filled follicles often look like a "string of pearls" along the edge of the ovary.

Common Signs & Symptoms to Watch For

PCOS is a spectrum condition. You do not need to experience every single symptom to have PCOS. The most frequent signs include:

  • Irregular or Skipped Periods: Menstrual cycles spaced farther than 35 days apart, fewer than 8 periods in a year, or cycles that are unusually prolonged or unpredictable.
  • Difficulty Managing Weight: Unexplained weight gain, particularly around the lower abdomen, along with significant difficulty losing weight despite standard dieting.
  • Hormonal Acne & Oily Skin: Deep, cystic breakouts primarily situated along the jawline, chin, and upper neck that resist standard over-the-counter creams.
  • Unwanted Facial & Body Hair (Hirsutism): Noticeable coarse, dark hair growth on the upper lip, chin, chest, or abdomen caused by excess androgens.
  • Scalp Hair Thinning: Androgenic alopecia, characterized by diffuse hair loss or thinning at the crown and parting of the scalp.
  • Darkening of Skin Folds (Acanthosis Nigricans): Velvety, darkened skin patches around the back of the neck, armpits, or groin area, which is a physical indicator of insulin resistance.
  • Ovulatory Subfertility: Taking longer to conceive due to infrequent or unpredictable ovulation.
  • Intense Sugar Cravings & Fatigue: Mid-day energy crashes and persistent cravings for carbohydrates and sweets.

💡 Important Clinical Note

Having irregular periods alone does not automatically guarantee PCOS. Other conditions, such as thyroid dysfunction (hypothyroidism) or elevated prolactin levels, can mimic similar symptoms. An accurate clinical diagnosis by an experienced gynaecologist is essential before beginning any medication.

Why Does PCOS Happen? The Core Drivers

While the exact genetic triggers continue to be studied, clinical medicine identifies three primary pillars driving PCOS:

1. Insulin Resistance

Insulin is the hormone that helps cells absorb glucose (sugar) from the bloodstream for energy. In approximately 70% of women with PCOS, the body's cells become resistant to insulin. The pancreas compensates by pumping out even more insulin. High levels of circulating insulin directly stimulate the ovaries to produce excess testosterone and suppress Sex Hormone-Binding Globulin (SHBG).

2. Hormonal Disruption (LH & Androgens)

An altered ratio between Luteinizing Hormone (LH) and Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) leads to irregular ovarian stimulation, hindering natural egg release.

3. Low-Grade Chronic Inflammation

Elevated inflammatory markers promote ovarian androgen secretion and compound insulin resistance, contributing to systemic fatigue and weight challenges.

Practical Steps You Can Take: Daily Lifestyle Strategies

Medical research consistently shows that lifestyle modifications are the first-line therapy for managing PCOS. Even a modest 5% to 10% reduction in body weight can restore spontaneous ovulation and significantly improve cycle regularity.

🥗 1. Eat for Blood Sugar Balance

  • Prioritize Low-Glycemic Complex Carbohydrates: Swap refined white rice and maida for traditional whole grains like jowar, bajra, brown rice, and whole oats.
  • Pair Carbs with Protein and Healthy Fats: Always combine carbs with lentils (dal), paneer, sprouts, eggs, or nuts. This slows down glucose absorption and prevents insulin spikes.
  • Ample Dietary Fiber: High-fiber vegetables (spinach, methi, cucumbers, carrots) help nourish the gut microbiome and facilitate hormonal clearance.
  • Limit Refined Sugars & Ultra-Processed Snacks: Minimize packaged soft drinks, bakery biscuits, and deep-fried snacks.

🏃‍♀️ 2. Smart, Consistent Movement

  • Aim for 30 to 45 minutes of moderate physical activity 4 to 5 days a week.
  • A combination of brisk walking, swimming, cycling, and resistance/strength training has been proven to increase muscle insulin sensitivity for up to 48 hours post-exercise.

😴 3. Prioritize Restorative Sleep & Stress Management

  • Chronic sleep deprivation (less than 7 hours per night) spikes the stress hormone cortisol, which directly worsens insulin resistance and food cravings.
  • Incorporate evening screen wind-downs, gentle stretching, or pranayama to calm the nervous system.

Medical Management at Sarda Hospital

While lifestyle changes form the cornerstone of management, personalized medical care provides the necessary support to regulate your cycles and safeguard your long-term health:

  • Pelvic Ultrasound: High-resolution sonography to evaluate ovarian volume, antral follicle count, and endometrial thickness.
  • Targeted Lab Panels: Screening of fasting blood sugar, HbA1c, thyroid (TSH), lipid profile, and hormonal markers.
  • Cycle Regulation: Gentle, tailored medical therapy to prevent endometrial hyperplasia (excessive uterine lining build-up) when periods are absent for several months.
  • Fertility & Pre-Conception Guidance: For couples trying to conceive, safe ovulation-induction protocols (such as letrozole) paired with precise follicular tracking help achieve healthy, natural pregnancies.

When Should You Consult a Gynaecologist?

Schedule a medical consultation if you notice:

  • Your periods have stopped for more than 3 months, or cycles exceed 45 days.
  • You experience unusually heavy, painful, or prolonged bleeding.
  • You have been actively trying to conceive for 6 to 12 months without success.
  • You are experiencing rapid, unexplained hair growth on the face or sudden severe acne.
